# Layout Patterns Reference

Use these layout patterns to avoid default AI/SaaS structure.

## Bad default to avoid

```text
Centered hero
↓
Subtitle
↓
Two CTA buttons
↓
Three equal feature cards
↓
Bento grid
↓
Testimonials
↓
FAQ
↓
Final CTA
```

This structure is not always wrong, but it is overused and often makes products look AI-generated.

## 1. Asymmetric Product Hero

### Use for

- SaaS landing pages,
- developer tools,
- productivity products,
- apps with strong product UI.

### Structure

- left: sharp product promise and action,
- right: oversized product screenshot or real UI state,
- below: proof strip or workflow preview.

### Why it works

It makes the product tangible and avoids abstract gradient marketing.

## 2. Command Center Layout

### Use for

- developer tools,
- admin systems,
- AI agents,
- internal tools,
- monitoring products.

### Structure

- top: active context/status,
- left: navigation or command list,
- center: main operational view,
- right: detail/evidence/config panel.

### Why it works

It communicates capability through real operational structure.

## 3. Editorial Split Layout

### Use for

- research products,
- premium products,
- thoughtful SaaS,
- privacy/security products.

### Structure

- one strong headline column,
- one narrative/proof column,
- source/evidence cards below,
- strong typographic rhythm.

### Why it works

It feels authored, not generated.

## 4. Workflow Timeline

### Use for

- automation tools,
- AI pipelines,
- onboarding flows,
- process-heavy products.

### Structure

- step-by-step workflow,
- each step has real input/output,
- status and decision points visible.

### Why it works

It explains the product through behavior, not generic feature claims.

## 5. Native Settings Window

### Use for

- macOS/desktop apps,
- utilities,
- privacy tools,
- local-first software.

### Structure

- compact title bar,
- sidebar categories,
- settings rows,
- status indicators,
- local model/provider controls.

### Why it works

It fits platform expectations and avoids web SaaS aesthetics.

## 6. Evidence Ledger Layout

### Use for

- accountability platforms,
- research archives,
- audit tools,
- promise/receipt databases.

### Structure

- entity header,
- score/explanation panel,
- evidence timeline,
- source table,
- right-of-reply/status module.

### Why it works

It prioritizes trust and traceability.

## 7. Dense Dashboard Layout

### Use for

- analytics,
- finance,
- monitoring,
- operational products.

### Structure

- KPI strip,
- filters,
- table/chart pair,
- drilldown drawer,
- audit/log section.

### Why it works

It replaces vague marketing with real information architecture.

## 8. One Big Object Layout

### Use for

- focused products,
- simple apps,
- launch pages,
- product demos.

### Structure

- one dominant screenshot/mockup/object,
- supporting annotations,
- minimal copy,
- one primary action.

### Why it works

It prevents feature-card clutter and creates memorability.

## 9. Problem / Evidence / Outcome

### Use for

- B2B products,
- serious SaaS,
- security/privacy tools,
- trust-heavy products.

### Structure

- problem with concrete symptom,
- evidence or example,
- product mechanism,
- outcome/proof.

### Why it works

It prevents vague “transform your workflow” language.

## 10. Product Storyboard

### Use for

- landing pages,
- onboarding,
- demo pages,
- AI workflow products.

### Structure

- user starts with messy input,
- product performs concrete action,
- user reviews/controls,
- output appears in target environment.

### Why it works

It shows causality and reduces generic claims.
